EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The U.S. economy keeps growing at a moderate but positive pace, with a labor market cooling in an orderly fashion and inflation still drifting down toward the Federal Reserve's 2 % target. The Fed has paused rate hikes and the market is pricing in one or two cuts over the next twelve months, which supports equities and eases the cost of credit. The main risks remain sticky services inflation, geopolitical tension around energy prices, and demanding equity valuations in the technology sector. Overall, the environment is constructive for long-term investors, though with less margin for error than in previous years: it pays to stay diversified and keep some cash on hand to take advantage of pullbacks.

💡 WHAT THIS MEANS FOR YOU

  • If you invest in stocks: a Fed on pause with cuts in sight usually favors equities, but tech valuations already price in good news — diversify and don't chase prices.
  • If you save: take advantage of rates while they're still high: high-yield savings accounts, CDs and Treasury bills still pay attractive returns that will fall once the Fed cuts.
  • If you have or want a mortgage/loans: the cost of credit should drift lower over the coming quarters; if you can wait or refinance later, you may get better terms.
  • For the labor market: hiring is cooling but not collapsing; wages are now growing faster than inflation, slowly restoring purchasing power.
